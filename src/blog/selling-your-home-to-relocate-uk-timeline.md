## Why the Timeline Matters More Than the Date

When a move depends on selling first, the actual moving date is often the last thing to become clear — it's set by how the sale progresses, not by a diary entry. Planning around a realistic timeline, rather than a hoped-for date, tends to make the whole process far less stressful.

## What a UK Sale-and-Move Timeline Usually Involves

- **Preparing the property** — sorting, decluttering and staging, ideally before it's listed rather than during viewings.
- **On the market** — [HM Land Registry data](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/land-registry) and industry reporting consistently show UK sale timelines vary widely by region and price point, which is exactly why a fixed moving date set too early tends to cause problems.
- **Under offer to exchange** — the stage most likely to see delays, particularly where a chain is involved.
- **Exchange to completion** — usually the most predictable part of the process, once everything above it is settled.

## Where Chains Add Uncertainty

A chain means your sale depends on someone else's purchase, which depends on someone else's sale — and any one link slowing down affects everyone behind it. This is one of the main reasons moves that involve both a sale and a purchase benefit from broader coordination, not just a removal booking.
