## The Part That Isn't Really About Packing

Sorting through decades of belongings is rarely difficult because of the physical task — it's difficult because so much of it carries memory. A downsize almost always includes a much harder decision underneath the practical one: what to keep, what to pass on, and what to finally let go of.

## Separate "Sorting" From "Deciding"

Trying to sort and decide everything's fate in one pass is what makes this feel overwhelming. It tends to work better in two stages: first, group belongings by category without deciding anything yet; then, go back through each group separately, with time to actually think about each one.

## Questions Worth Asking, Room by Room

- Has this been used or displayed in the last two years, or has it been in a box already?
- Would a family member want this specifically, or is it being kept out of habit?
- Could this be photographed and kept as a memory, without keeping the physical item?
- Is there a smaller version of this that would serve the same purpose in a new home?

## Involve Family, But Don't Let Them Decide For You

Adult children and grandchildren are often glad to take specific items, and asking early avoids anything being let go of that someone would have wanted. That said, the decision belongs to the person downsizing — well-meaning family input works best as information, not pressure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long should I allow for sorting sentimental items?**
Longer than feels necessary — starting 2 to 3 months before a move gives enough room to make considered decisions rather than rushed ones.

**Can Chayce help with this before a moving date is even set?**
Yes. Home sorting and declutter support can begin well ahead of a confirmed date, and often should.
