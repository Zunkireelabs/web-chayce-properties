**Short answer:** downsize in stages. Decide where you are going, measure what will fit, sort belongings into keep, sell, donate and let-go piles by category, and start 6 to 8 weeks before moving day (longer for a family home). The checklist below follows that order.

## Why downsizing feels different from an ordinary move

Downsizing a long-time family home is about deciding what comes with you into the next chapter. That decision often carries more emotional weight than the move itself, and it is the part that is hardest to fit around a fixed removal date. Most problems come from having a date but no sequence of steps, so a checklist helps more than a deadline.

## Step-by-step downsizing checklist

1. **Decide roughly where you are going and when.** Knowing the type and size of the new home gives every later decision a purpose. If you are still deciding, see [when is the right time to downsize](/blog/when-to-downsize-uk/) and [downsizing to a bungalow](/blog/downsizing-to-a-bungalow-uk/).
2. **Get the new home's floor plan.** Measure rooms, doorways and stairs before you decide on furniture. Our guide to [planning furniture and a floor plan for a smaller home](/blog/planning-furniture-and-floor-plan-for-a-smaller-home/) shows how.
3. **List what genuinely matters.** Write down the items with real use or real meaning to you before you start judging everything else.
4. **Set a timeline.** Allow 6 to 8 weeks for sorting at the very least. Start earlier than feels necessary, since rushed decisions are the ones people regret.
5. **Sort by category, not room.** Clothing, books, kitchenware and paperwork are easier to judge when you can see everything of one type together.
6. **Use four piles** (below) and a "decide later" box.
7. **Do the sentimental items separately** and unhurriedly. See [sentimental belongings when downsizing](/blog/sentimental-belongings-when-downsizing/).
8. **Arrange sale, donation and collection** for everything that is leaving, with a firm deadline.
9. **Pack by destination room** and label boxes so everything can be placed straight away.
10. **Keep the final week light.** Dates move, so finish sorting well before the removal.

## The four piles

**Keep:** things you use regularly, or that will genuinely fit and matter in your next home.

**Sell:** items with real value that are not sentimental, such as furniture, electronics and collectables.

**Donate:** anything in good condition that someone else could use.

**Let go:** worn out, broken or duplicated items that serve no purpose.

If you haven't used or thought about an item in the last 12 months, it is usually a candidate for sell, donate or let go. The exceptions are seasonal items, hobby equipment and things with sentimental weight such as photographs, letters and inherited pieces. Those deserve their own pass, not a rushed decision the week before moving.

## Furniture and bulky items

Place large furniture on paper first, then decide what leaves. Offer pieces to family before selling, and choose between sale, donation and collection based on how much time you have. Our guide on [planning furniture and a floor plan](/blog/planning-furniture-and-floor-plan-for-a-smaller-home/) covers the options for what does not fit.

## Paperwork and money

Keep important papers such as deeds, insurance, pension and identity documents in one clearly marked box that travels with you. Selling a home, tax and benefits can all be affected by a move. This is general information, not financial or legal advice, so speak to a qualified adviser about your own circumstances.

## Check the money before you sort

Before you start clearing, it helps to know the financial picture. [Independent Age](https://www.independentage.org/get-advice/housing/housing-options/downsizing) notes that moving costs can be high, that tax may be payable when you buy, and that proceeds from selling your home can affect means-tested benefits. Independent Age also suggests checking storage space for your belongings, how easily family and friends can visit, and the local facilities and transport at the new home.
