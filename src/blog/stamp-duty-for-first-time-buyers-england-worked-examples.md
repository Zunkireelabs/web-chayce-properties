**Short answer:** in England, first-time buyers pay no Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) up to £300,000 and 5% on the part of the price between £300,001 and £500,000. If the home costs more than £500,000, the relief is lost and the standard rates apply to the whole price. This guide shows the sums. It is general information, not tax advice.

## The rules in brief

According to [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/stamp-duty-land-tax/residential-property-rates) on 13 October 2026:

- You can claim first-time buyer relief if you and anyone you are buying with are all first-time buyers.
- There is no SDLT up to £300,000.
- There is 5% SDLT on the portion from £300,001 to £500,000.
- If the price is over £500,000, you cannot claim the relief.

The standard rates for a single home are 0% up to £125,000, 2% from £125,001 to £250,000, 5% from £250,001 to £925,000, 10% from £925,001 to £1.5 million and 12% above that. Tax is charged band by band, not on the whole price at one rate.

## Worked examples for England

| Price of the home | Standard rates | First-time buyer relief | You save |
|---|---|---|---|
| £200,000 | £1,500 | £0 | £1,500 |
| £300,000 | £5,000 | £0 | £5,000 |
| £350,000 | £7,500 | £2,500 | £5,000 |
| £400,000 | £10,000 | £5,000 | £5,000 |
| £450,000 | £12,500 | £7,500 | £5,000 |
| £500,000 | £15,000 | £10,000 | £5,000 |
| £510,000 | £15,500 | £15,500 (relief lost) | £0 |

How to read the £400,000 row: with the relief, nothing is due on the first £300,000, and 5% of the remaining £100,000 is £5,000. At the standard rates the same home costs £10,000, so the relief saves £5,000.

## The £500,000 cliff

The relief stops completely above £500,000. A £500,000 home costs £10,000 in SDLT with the relief, but a £510,000 home costs £15,500, because the standard rates then apply to the whole price. If you are close to the cap, speak to your solicitor before you agree a price.
