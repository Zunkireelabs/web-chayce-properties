**Short answer:** in the UK, sheltered housing gives light support and independence, assisted living (also called extra-care housing) gives more care while you keep your own flat, and a care home provides accommodation and personal care for people who need extra support. Staying at home with adaptations is another option. The right one depends on the support needed now and in future, so start with a care needs assessment from your local council.

*General information from the sources listed below, not care, legal or financial advice. Rules differ in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, and charges vary by scheme.*

## The four options at a glance

| | Staying at home (adapted) | Sheltered housing | Assisted living (extra care) | Care home |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | Your own home, changed to fit your needs, with help arranged as required | Housing you can buy or rent, with a scheme manager, emergency alarm and communal areas | A self-contained flat with its own front door and on-site staff | Accommodation and personal care for people who need extra support in daily life |
| Support | Arranged separately, such as home care | Scheme manager (warden), 24-hour emergency alarm, social activities | Staff usually available up to 24 hours a day, an alarm and social activities | Staff provide personal care and support |
| Personal care | Arranged separately | Not usually provided, but you can arrange it through the council or a private agency | Help with washing, dressing, the toilet and medication; domestic help and meals may be provided | Provided |
| Inspected and rated? | Not applicable | No | Yes, by the Care Quality Commission (CQC) | Yes |
| Buy, rent or placed | Your own home | Usually buy or rent, with a minimum age such as 55 or 60 | Buy or rent privately, or from the council after a care needs assessment; not in every area | Arranged privately or through the council |
| First step | A council needs assessment; a Disabled Facilities Grant may help in England | Ask the scheme what support is on offer and what every charge covers | A care needs assessment if the council is involved; otherwise ask the scheme | A care needs assessment from your local council |
| Main thing to check | Cost and disruption of adaptations, and whether the home adapts well | Ongoing charges as well as upfront costs, and the level of support | All charges: rent or ground rent, care and service charges | Funding, and the latest inspection report |

These are typical patterns taken from the sources below. Always check the details of the specific scheme or home.

## Staying at home with adaptations

Many people want to stay in the home and community they know. Adaptations such as grab rails, a stairlift or a level-access shower can make that possible, and your local council can arrange an assessment of your needs. In England, a means-tested [Disabled Facilities Grant](https://www.gov.uk/disabled-facilities-grants) can help with larger adaptations. Our guide to [adapting a home or moving in later life](/blog/stay-or-move-adapting-a-home-or-moving-in-later-life-uk/) compares the two honestly.

## Sheltered housing

Sheltered housing, sometimes called retirement housing, is housing you can buy or rent that has support such as a scheme manager, emergency alarms and social activities. It suits people who want to live independently in a smaller home that is easier to manage, with reassurance if help is needed. Age UK notes that schemes offer different levels of support, so be clear on what is available. Our page on [moving into sheltered housing](/moving-into-sheltered-housing/) and our guide to [retirement housing options](/blog/moving-into-a-retirement-village-uk-guide/) cover the practical side.

## Assisted living (extra-care housing)

Assisted living is more than sheltered housing and less than a care home. You live in your own self-contained flat, and staff are usually available up to 24 hours a day for personal care and support that is tailored to you. It is regulated by the CQC, which inspects it and publishes ratings. Some extra-care housing is available to buy or rent privately, and some comes from the council after a care needs assessment, but it is not available in every area.

## Care homes

A care home, also known as a residential home, provides accommodation and personal care for people who need extra support in their daily lives. Care homes are inspected and rated, so check the latest report for any home you are considering on the [CQC website](https://www.cqc.org.uk/care-services). Funding is a separate question; our guide to [who pays for a care home](/blog/who-pays-for-a-care-home-uk-where-to-get-advice/) points to the right places for advice.

## How to choose

1. **Ask for a care needs assessment** from your local council. It is the first step to arranging any kind of care.
2. **Think about the next five years,** not only today. Ask whether a change in needs would mean a second move.
3. **Compare every charge,** including service charges, ground rent and care costs, not only the headline price.
4. **Visit more than once,** at different times of day, and speak to current residents where you can.
5. **Take independent advice** from [Age UK](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/information-advice/care/housing-options/), your council or a regulated adviser before committing money or signing a lease.
