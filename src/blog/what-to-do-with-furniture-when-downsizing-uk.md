**Short answer:** when downsizing in the UK, decide first what fits in the new home, then sell or give away reusable furniture, for example to a charity shop. For anything nobody wants, ask your council about special collections for large items such as sofas, use the recycling centre, or use a private business that is on the register of waste carriers. You can be fined if you give your waste to someone who is not legally allowed to take it.

*General information from GOV.UK. Council services, charges and rules vary, so check your own council's website.*

## Start with what fits

Do not decide what to get rid of until you know what the new home can hold. Measure the rooms, draw a simple floor plan and choose the pieces that matter most. Our guide to [planning furniture for a smaller home](/blog/planning-furniture-and-floor-plan-for-a-smaller-home/) shows how, and our [downsizing checklist](/blog/downsizing-checklist-what-to-keep-uk/) covers the keep, sell, donate and let-go piles.

## Your options

| Option | Good for | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Keep and move | Pieces that fit the new home and matter to you | Measure the new rooms first |
| Sell | Furniture in good condition | Start early, so it is gone before moving day |
| Give away or donate | Reusable items | A charity shop is one option GOV.UK lists; if a charity collects, check it is on the register of waste carriers |
| Council special collection | Large items such as sofas or fridges | Check your council's website for what it collects |
| Recycling centre (the tip) | Items you can take yourself | Find yours on your council's website |
| Private clearance or skip firm | Larger volumes | It must be on the register of waste carriers |

## Check before anything leaves the house

Your household waste must be disposed of correctly. GOV.UK says that if you give it to someone else, you must make sure they are legally allowed to take it, and you could be fined if they are not.

- **A private business** such as a skip company or house clearance service must be on the [register of waste carriers](https://environment.data.gov.uk/public-register/view/search-waste-carriers-brokers). Select upper tier. Wales, Northern Ireland and Scotland have their own registers.
- **A charity that collects** from your home must be on the same register. Select lower tier.
- **A private waste site** must have an environmental permit or a registered waste exemption. Ask for the number.

If waste is dumped illegally, you can [report fly-tipping](https://www.gov.uk/report-flytipping) on GOV.UK.

## If the belongings are part of an estate

When furniture belonged to someone who has died, it can form part of the estate. Check with the executor or solicitor before anything is given away, sold or thrown out. Our guide to [clearing a parent's house after a death](/blog/moving-after-probate-clearing-a-loved-ones-home/) sets out the steps.

## Give yourself time

Sorting is usually the slowest part of downsizing. Our guide suggests allowing at least 6 to 8 weeks for sorting alone, and longer for a large family home, so there is time to sell, donate and book collections.
