**Short answer:** moving an elderly parent into your home can work well in the UK, but agree the practical and financial questions first. Age UK suggests asking what care your parent will need and who will give it, whether the home needs adapting, how it will be paid for, whether benefits or Council Tax are affected, and what happens if it stops working. Get independent legal advice and consider a formal agreement.

*General information from Age UK and GOV.UK, not care, legal, tax or financial advice.*

## Questions to settle before anyone moves

Age UK sets out the questions to ask when moving in with family. In plain terms:

- **Care.** What sort of care will your parent need, and who will be able to provide it?
- **The home.** Will it need adapting? Age UK's guide to [adapting your home](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/information-advice/care/housing-options/adapting-home/) is a good start, and in England a means-tested [Disabled Facilities Grant](https://www.gov.uk/disabled-facilities-grants) may help with larger adaptations.
- **Money.** How will it be paid for? Will your parent pay rent or help towards bills?
- **Benefits.** If family members are claiming benefits, will they be affected?
- **Property.** If your parent invests in your property or buys one jointly, it could matter if they later have a financial assessment for help with care home fees or benefits.
- **Council Tax.** Moving into a family member's home could affect Council Tax, so get advice first. See [GOV.UK on Council Tax](https://www.gov.uk/council-tax), or call Age UK's free Advice Line.
- **If it does not work out.** What would happen if your parent could not live there any more, for example if it does not work out or they need to move into a care home?

## Get it in writing

Age UK advises getting independent legal advice and considering a formal agreement. It can feel overly formal when it is family, but being clear from the start is better for everyone if things do not go to plan. Our guide to [Lasting Power of Attorney](/blog/lasting-power-of-attorney-moving-a-parent/) explains who can make decisions if your parent cannot.

## Have a plan B

Moving in is rarely permanent. Know what the other options look like before you need them, so a change in needs does not become a crisis. Our comparison of [care home, assisted living, sheltered housing or staying put](/blog/care-home-assisted-living-sheltered-housing-or-staying-put-uk/) sets them side by side, and our guide to [who pays for a care home](/blog/who-pays-for-a-care-home-uk-where-to-get-advice/) points to the right places for funding advice.

## The practical move

Moving a parent in means two homes to deal with: sorting and downsizing theirs, and making room in yours. It helps to decide early what comes with them and what does not.
