## Why Decluttering First Makes the Whole Move Easier

Few jobs feel as daunting as deciding what to do with a lifetime of belongings before a move. Yet decluttering before you pack is the single step that makes everything afterwards simpler. Every item you decide not to take is one less thing to wrap, carry, insure, store and unpack at the other end — and in a smaller home, space you don't have to find. The temptation is to box it all and sort it later, but "later" usually arrives in the new house, where you are tired, surrounded by cartons, and no better placed to make the decision than you were weeks earlier.

For older people, and for families arranging a move on their behalf, the task carries extra weight. A home lived in for decades holds not just furniture but memories, and that makes letting go genuinely hard — not a matter of tidiness but of time and feeling. The good news is that decluttering done early, at a gentle pace, turns an overwhelming pile into a series of small, manageable decisions. It also tends to lower the final removal cost, because you are moving less. ## Start Small: A Room-by-Room Method

The quickest way to lose momentum is to try to tackle the whole house at once. Instead, choose a single room — ideally one with low emotional charge, such as a spare room, utility cupboard or garage — and finish it before moving on. Early wins build confidence for the harder spaces later.

Within each room, a simple three-way sort works well:

1. **Keep** — things that fit your next home and the way you want to live there.
2. **Pass on** — items in good condition that can go to family, a charity or be sold.
3. **Let go** — things that are broken, duplicated or simply no longer needed.

Keep the boxes or bags physically separate so decisions don't creep back. Work in short sessions rather than marathon days; an hour at a time is far more sustainable, especially where mobility, energy or sentiment make the going slow. Leave the most personal categories — photographs, letters, keepsakes — until last, once you've found your rhythm. ## Deciding What to Keep When It All Feels Important

The hardest part of decluttering is rarely the obvious rubbish — it's the things that mean something. A useful shift is to judge each item against the home you are moving to, not the one you are leaving. Will it fit the room? Does it suit how you'll actually live day to day? Is it worth the effort and cost of moving it? When something only earns its place out of habit, that is usually a sign it can move on.

Sentiment still deserves respect. You don't have to keep an entire dinner service to keep the memory of family meals — one piece, or a photograph of it, can carry the feeling while freeing the space. Passing things to children, grandchildren or a cause you care about often feels far better than discarding them. ## Frequently asked questions

### When should I start decluttering before a move?

As early as you can — ideally weeks before, not days. Working a little at a time, one room per session, keeps decisions calm rather than rushed and avoids the last-minute pressure of packing everything at once.

### What is the easiest way to decide what to keep?

Measure against your next home, not your current one. Ask whether an item fits the space, suits how you'll live there, and is worth the cost and effort of moving. If it only earns a place out of habit, it can usually be passed on.
