Short answer: the best time to downsize is when you can still make unhurried decisions. Many people wait for a crisis, but a planned move, started three to six months ahead, is calmer and gives you more choice over where you go and what you keep.

## Common signs it may be time

- Rooms are closed off or only used for storage.
- The house or garden takes more time, money or energy than you want to give.
- Stairs, baths or steep paths are becoming harder.
- You want to be nearer family, friends, shops or healthcare.
- You would like to release some value from the home.

None of these means you must move. They are prompts to start thinking and planning.

## Why earlier is often easier

Sorting a lifetime of belongings takes time. Doing it early means you can make considered choices, involve family and avoid selling or letting go of things in a hurry. It also gives you more options on where to live.

## Check the money side before you decide

Independent Age lists the main upsides of downsizing as [releasing capital, lower running costs and less maintenance](https://www.independentage.org/get-advice/housing/housing-options/downsizing). It also warns that moving costs can be high, that a smaller home is not automatically cheaper, that tax may be payable on the purchase, and that money from selling your home could affect eligibility for means-tested benefits. If benefits matter to you, take advice before you sell. Independent Age runs a free helpline on 0800 319 6789.

## What to do first

1. Decide roughly where you would like to live and why.
2. Talk to family about what you want.
3. Start sorting the least emotional rooms.
4. Get a plan, timeline and budget. A [Discovery Consultation](/discovery/), £150, gives you all three with no obligation to go further.
