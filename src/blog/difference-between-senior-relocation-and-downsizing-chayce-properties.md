**Short answer:** downsizing means moving to a smaller property, often with fewer bedrooms or less floor space, to simplify life and reduce upkeep. Senior relocation is the wider process of moving to a home that suits later-life needs, which may or may not be smaller.

## What downsizing means

Downsizing is about the size of the home. You move to a smaller place to cut maintenance and keep life simple.

People often start thinking about it when:

- rooms are closed off or only used for storage;
- the house or garden takes more time, money or energy than you want to give;
- stairs, baths or steep paths are becoming harder;
- you would like to release some value from the home.

Independent Age lists the main upsides of downsizing as [releasing capital, lower running costs and less maintenance](https://www.independentage.org/get-advice/housing/housing-options/downsizing).

## What senior relocation means

Senior relocation is about fit. It focuses on finding accommodation that supports changing circumstances, such as being closer to family, friends, shops or healthcare, having less to look after, or having specialist facilities.

A relocation may involve a smaller space, or it may not. For some people the right answer is a different area rather than fewer rooms. ## Downsizing and senior relocation at a glance

| | Downsizing | Senior relocation |
|---|---|---|
| Main aim | Fewer rooms and less to look after | A home and place that suit later-life needs |
| Size of the new home | Smaller | Smaller, the same or different, depending on need |
| The question it answers | How much home do I need? | Where will I live well? |
| Typical reasons | Upkeep, running costs, unused rooms | Being near family, shops or healthcare; easier access; specialist facilities |
| Always involved | Sorting and decluttering belongings | Choosing where to live, plus sorting and the move itself |

## When it is both

The two often go together. A move to a smaller home that is also nearer family, easier to manage and closer to healthcare is downsizing and relocating at once. The useful question is not which label fits, but what the move needs to achieve for you.

## Which is right for you?

Start with the problem you are trying to solve:

1. Is the main difficulty the size and upkeep of the home, or where it is?
2. Would you like to be nearer family, friends, shops or healthcare?
3. Are stairs, baths or steep paths making daily life harder?
4. Do you need to release money from the home, and have you checked what that could mean for you?

Independent Age also warns that moving costs can be high, that a smaller home is not automatically cheaper, that tax may be payable on the purchase, and that money from selling your home could affect eligibility for means-tested benefits. If benefits matter to you, take advice before you sell.

## What both kinds of move involve

Whichever you choose, the same jobs usually need to happen in the right order:

- deciding where to live next, and whether to move at all;
- sorting and decluttering a home that may hold decades of belongings;
- timing the move around a sale or a purchase (see our [UK timeline for selling and relocating](/blog/selling-your-home-to-relocate-uk-timeline/));
- packing, the removal itself, and setting up the new home.

Allow three to six months where you can. A planned move is calmer and gives you more choice than one made in a hurry. Our [downsizing checklist](/blog/downsizing-checklist-what-to-keep-uk/) and guide to [when to downsize](/blog/when-to-downsize-uk/) help you get started.
