Short answer: retirement villages, retirement flats and sheltered housing all let older people live independently in their own home, but they differ in size, services, ownership and cost. Work out which type suits you, ask detailed questions about charges and rules before committing, and plan the move around the building's access and move-in dates. This guide covers all three and ends with a checklist.

This is general information, not legal or financial advice. Before buying, renting or signing a lease, take advice from a solicitor and, where relevant, a financial adviser.

## The three main types at a glance

| | Retirement village | Retirement flat or apartment | Sheltered housing |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | A community of independent homes (flats and bungalows) on one site | A flat in a building built or converted for older residents | A flat or bungalow in a smaller scheme, often with a scheme manager |
| Shared facilities | Often a café or restaurant, lounges, gardens, sometimes a gym or health centre | Usually a residents' lounge, lift and communal gardens | Usually a shared lounge and laundry |
| On-site help | Staff or house manager, emergency call system, sometimes care on site | Often a manager or emergency call system | Scheme manager or support arrangements, emergency call |
| Typical tenure | Leasehold purchase, or rent | Leasehold purchase, or rent | Often rented from a housing association or council; some are bought |
| Main thing to check | Service charge and exit fees | Lease terms and ground rent | Eligibility, rent and service charges |

These are typical patterns, not rules. Always check the details of the specific development.

## Retirement village

A retirement village is a self-contained community of independent homes built for older residents, with shared facilities on site. You have your own front door. It is not a care home: personal care is not provided as standard, though some villages offer care on the same site.

What to check:

- **Fees.** Ask what the monthly service charge covers, how it is set and how it can rise. Ask about any fee payable when you sell or leave, and what happens to the home when it is sold.
- **Care.** Can care be added on site, and on what terms? If not, would a change in needs mean a second move?
- **Healthcare.** Is there an on-site health service, or do you rely on local GP surgeries?
- **The community.** Visit more than once, at different times of day, and speak to current residents if you can. Ask whether facilities shown in the brochure are finished and open.
- **Rules.** Pets, guests, parking, age limits for a partner and whether you can sub-let.

## Retirement flat or apartment

A retirement flat is a self-contained home in a building designed or converted for older people. Services are usually lighter than in a village, and the details vary a great deal.

What to check:

- **Lease.** Length, ground rent, service charge and any resale restrictions. A solicitor should read this before you commit.
- **Moving-in rules.** Delivery windows, lift booking, parking for the removal vehicle and any restrictions on days or times.
- **Storage and access.** Is there a lift? Is there a bike, scooter or storage room? How far is the parking from the door?
- **Facilities.** Which communal areas are included in the charge, and who manages the building?

## Sheltered housing

Sheltered housing is designed for older people who want to live independently with some support nearby. It commonly means a flat or bungalow in a scheme with shared areas and a scheme manager or support arrangements. Many schemes are run by housing associations or councils, and some are available to buy.

What to check:

- **Eligibility and cost.** Who can apply, how allocation works, and what the rent or purchase price and service charges are.
- **Support.** What the scheme manager or support worker does, and when they are on site.
- **Rules.** Pets, guests, overnight visitors, parking, and what furniture and appliances you can bring.
- **Move-in date.** Whether you can measure up before moving in, and how much notice you get.

## Retirement housing is one of several options

[Age UK's housing options guide](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/documents/en-gb/information-guides/ageukig08_housing_options_inf.pdf) sets out the choices side by side: staying put, moving to another home, or moving somewhere with more support such as sheltered housing, extra care housing or a care home. [Independent Age](https://www.independentage.org/get-advice/housing/housing-options/downsizing) also points to housing designed for older people, with features such as step-free access, and to moving in with family or friends, which needs careful talk about money, care and legal protection.

## Questions to ask whichever type you choose

1. What exactly do the service or management charges cover, and how have they changed in recent years?
2. What happens to costs, and to the home, if I leave, sell or need more care?
3. Can I see the lease, contract or tenancy agreement before I decide?
4. What are the moving-in dates and rules, and can I get in early to measure?
5. What support is there out of hours, and how does the emergency call work?
6. What can I bring, in terms of furniture, appliances and pets?

Ask for answers in writing, and keep copies.

## Moving checklist

### Eight to six weeks before

- Confirm your move-in date in writing and ask the building about moving-in rules: delivery windows, lift booking and parking.
- Get a floor plan or measure each room. Decide which large furniture will fit before deciding anything else.
- Start sorting into keep, sell, donate and let go. Our [downsizing checklist](/blog/downsizing-checklist-what-to-keep-uk/) and guide to [planning furniture for a smaller home](/blog/planning-furniture-and-floor-plan-for-a-smaller-home/) help with this.

### Four to two weeks before

- Book your removal, and confirm the insurance details in writing. See [questions to ask a removals company](/blog/choosing-removals-company-for-elderly-person-uk/).
- Pack non-essentials and label boxes by destination room.
- Tell your bank, GP, pension provider and other services your new address, and arrange Royal Mail redirection.
- Tell the building about any large deliveries.

### The final week

- Pack a first-night box with a kettle, medication, chargers, bedding and toiletries.
- Confirm access and timings with the removal team and the building.
- Take meter readings at the old property.

### Moving day and settling in

Do a final walk round the old home, check the new keys, and make the bed first so you can rest. See [settling in after your move](/blog/settling-in-after-your-move/) for ideas for the first weeks.

## Planning for less space

Most retirement homes are smaller than a long-time family house, so this move nearly always includes a downsizing decision: what fits, what to let go of, and what matters enough to bring.
