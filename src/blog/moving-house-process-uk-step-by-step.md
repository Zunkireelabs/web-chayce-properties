**Short answer:** in England and Wales, moving house follows a set order: agree a price and an offer, appoint a solicitor or conveyancer, arrange searches and surveys, exchange contracts, then complete and move out. GOV.UK says buying or selling a home takes about 5 months on average, and longer in a chain. An offer is not legally binding until contracts are exchanged.

*General information from GOV.UK, not legal or financial advice. Scotland and Northern Ireland have different processes.*

## The Steps in Order

1. **Decide your budget.** If buying, decide how much to borrow and choose a mortgage lender. See [the cost of moving house](/blog/cost-of-moving-house-uk/).
2. **Choose a property, or prepare yours to sell.** When selling you will need the right paperwork, including an Energy Performance Certificate, and a selling price.
3. **Agree an offer.** Accept or negotiate an offer when selling, or make one when buying.
4. **Appoint a solicitor or conveyancer** to do the legal work.
5. **Arrange searches and surveys** when buying.
6. **Exchange contracts.** The agreement becomes legally binding.
7. **Complete.** Money and ownership transfer, and you move out and in.
8. **Move.** Book your removals team for the completion date, and follow our [moving day checklist](/blog/moving-day-checklist-uk/).

## When You Are Selling

GOV.UK sets out what selling involves: check how much you owe on your mortgage, find out what your home is worth, decide whether to use an estate agent, choose a solicitor or conveyancer, give buyers the right paperwork, decide a price, accept or negotiate an offer, exchange contracts and then complete. Our guide to [selling your home to relocate](/blog/selling-your-home-to-relocate-uk-timeline/) explains how chains add uncertainty.

## When You Are Buying

GOV.UK lists the steps: decide how much you need to borrow, choose a mortgage lender and a property, make an offer, exchange contracts, pay tax, arrange and pay for searches and surveys, and arrange for a removals company to help you move. If you are buying in England you may owe Stamp Duty Land Tax. See [GOV.UK's residential rates](https://www.gov.uk/stamp-duty-land-tax/residential-property-rates) and our guide to [stamp duty when downsizing](/blog/stamp-duty-when-downsizing-uk/).

## Exchange of Contracts

After an offer is accepted, a legal contract is drawn up to transfer ownership. GOV.UK says it covers the sale price, the property boundaries, which fixtures and fittings are included, any legal restrictions and rights, planning restrictions, services to the property and when the sale will complete. When both sides are happy they sign final copies and send them to each other. From then, the agreement is legally binding.

## Completion Day

Once contracts are exchanged and any remaining checks are done, the buyer's solicitor transfers the money. The seller's solicitor then pays off the mortgage, any secured debts, stamp duty if the seller is buying another property, estate agent fees, service charges and their own fee, and sends the balance to the seller. You must leave the property in the state agreed in the contract when you move out.

## Protect Yourself From Fraud

GOV.UK warns there is a risk of fraud when buying a home, because large sums of money are transferred. Check that you are speaking to or emailing the person or business you think it is, transfer money only to the correct bank accounts, and do not reveal too much personal information on social media.
