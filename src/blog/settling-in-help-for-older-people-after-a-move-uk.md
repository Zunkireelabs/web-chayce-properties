Short answer: settling in after a move in later life goes best when the home is set up before you arrive, familiar belongings are visible straight away, daily routines carry on, and someone is on hand in the first few weeks.

## Set up the home first

If possible, have the bed made, the kitchen working and the bathroom ready before you arrive, with a clearly labelled first night box. Our [first-week checklist](/blog/what-to-do-after-moving-house-uk-first-week-checklist/) sets out what to do on day one.

## Make it feel familiar

Put photographs, a favourite chair and bedding in place early. Keep the layout simple and clear of trailing wires and loose rugs. Our guide to [settling in after your move](/blog/settling-in-after-your-move/) has more practical ideas, and our guide to [floor plans for a smaller home](/blog/planning-furniture-and-floor-plan-for-a-smaller-home/) helps with furniture.

## Keep routines going

Meals, walks, hobbies and phone calls with family give the days a shape. If you have moved area, find the nearest shop, pharmacy and GP early.

## Meet people gradually

Say hello to neighbours, visit a local group or café, and ask family to visit in the first weeks. Age UK and local community groups run activities in many areas. Find out more from [Age UK](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/).

## When to ask for more support

If you are worried about how someone is coping after a move, talk to their GP or your local council's adult social care team. Our guide on [moving a parent with dementia](/blog/moving-house-with-dementia-in-the-family/) covers extra considerations.
