Short answer: in the UK, who pays for a care home depends on the person's care needs, their savings and property, and where they live. Some people pay for their own care, some get help from their local council after an assessment, and a few qualify for NHS-funded care. The rules differ across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, so start with your council and independent advice, not with a price list.

This guide only points you to the right places. It is not financial, legal or care advice, and it does not set out current thresholds or rules, which change.

## The main routes

- **Paying for your own care.** Some people fund a care home themselves, using savings, pensions or the value of their home. This is often called self-funding.
- **Help from the council.** A local council can assess a person's care needs, and may then look at their finances to decide whether it will contribute towards the cost.
- **NHS-funded care.** In some cases, where a person has significant ongoing health needs, the NHS may pay for care. This is decided by an assessment, not by the choice of home.
- **A mix.** Some people receive part of their care funded and top up the rest themselves.

## Where to start

1. **Ask your local council.** Its adult social care team can explain how assessments work and what happens next. You can find your council on [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/find-local-council).
2. **Talk to a free advice service.** [Age UK](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/), [Independent Age](https://www.independentage.org/) and [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/) provide free, independent information on care and money.
3. **Speak to your GP or the hospital team** if the move follows a health event. They can explain what health assessments may apply. See the [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/) for general information.
4. **Consider regulated financial advice** for complex decisions, such as using the value of a home to pay for care. Choose an adviser who specialises in later life.
