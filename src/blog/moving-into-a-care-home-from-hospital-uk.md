**Short answer:** moving from hospital into a care home is a decision made with the hospital team, the council and the family, not just a move. Age UK explains that staff should assess needs first and consider whether intermediate care or reablement could help before a long-term choice. If a care home is chosen, the practical move comes next: agree the move-in date with the home, sort what comes with the person and set the room up.

*General information from Age UK, not medical, care or legal advice. Rules and costs vary by area.*

## What Happens Before Someone Leaves Hospital

Age UK explains that many hospitals now use a discharge to assess approach. An initial assessment makes sure the person leaves safely, and an assessment of long-term needs follows after a period of recovery. There is no longer a requirement to carry out a care needs assessment before leaving hospital. Staff discuss needs with the person and agree a recovery and support plan.

## When a Care Home May Be Considered

If there is a possibility of living in a care home permanently after a stay in hospital, Age UK says staff should assess needs and consider whether the person would benefit from intermediate care and reablement. These services aim to let the person make as full a recovery as possible and regain confidence before a decision about long-term care.

- **Intermediate care** is short-term support that aims to help the person be as independent as possible. It can be provided in a community hospital, a care home or at home.
- **Reablement** is a form of intermediate care that helps the person learn or relearn skills needed for daily living.

## Costs and Timing

If the person is eligible, Age UK says they will receive up to 6 weeks of reablement care after discharge for free. If care is needed for longer than six weeks, they might have to pay. If they are unlikely to benefit further, staff carry out a care needs assessment to see whether long-term social care or NHS support is needed, and should consider NHS Continuing Healthcare. Ask the hospital team and your council's adult social care team what applies and what any service costs. Our guide to [who pays for a care home](/blog/who-pays-for-a-care-home-uk-where-to-get-advice/) points to the right places for advice.

## Choosing and Checking a Care Home

Check the latest inspection report for any home on the [Care Quality Commission website](https://www.cqc.org.uk/care-services), and visit if you can. Age UK's guide to [care homes](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/information-advice/care/arranging-care/care-homes/) covers choosing one and the types available.

## The Practical Move

Once the decision is made:

- Agree the move-in date, access and parking with the home, and ask what the room contains.
- Sort what comes with the person. A care home room is much smaller than a family home. Our [care home moving checklist](/blog/moving-an-elderly-person-into-a-care-home-checklist/) covers what to bring and label.
- Age UK suggests bringing photos and soft furnishings to make the room their own, telling staff about the person's routine and interests, and keeping in touch with family and friends. See [Age UK on moving into a care home](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/information-advice/care/arranging-care/care-homes/moving-into-care-home/).
- Decide what happens to the person's home. See [moving after a bereavement](/moving-after-bereavement/) if that is part of the picture.
