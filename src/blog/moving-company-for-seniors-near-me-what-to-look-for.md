Short answer: a good moving company for seniors does more than move boxes. Look for packing included, one named coordinator, DBS-checked staff, insurance in writing and patience with sorting and timing, then compare at least two written quotes.

## What "senior-friendly" should mean in practice

Many firms say they help older people. These are the things worth checking:

- **Packing included or offered,** so you are not lifting or deciding on the day. See [how a full-service removals company works](/blog/what-does-a-full-service-removals-company-do/).
- **One point of contact** instead of several companies to manage.
- **Vetted staff.** Ask whether they are DBS-checked (our guide to [DBS-checked movers](/blog/dbs-checked-movers-uk-what-to-ask/) lists the questions).
- **Insurance** that is fully explained and written down.
- **Flexible timing** if a sale date moves.
- **Help at the other end,** such as unpacking and setting up. Our guide to [settling-in help](/blog/settling-in-help-for-older-people-after-a-move-uk/) explains what this can include.

## Local or national?

A local mover can visit your home and knows the area. But many later-life moves are to be near family or a retirement property elsewhere, so check the company can handle your route. The [British Association of Removers](https://bar.co.uk/) lists member companies, and [Age UK](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/) gives independent advice on avoiding rogue traders.
