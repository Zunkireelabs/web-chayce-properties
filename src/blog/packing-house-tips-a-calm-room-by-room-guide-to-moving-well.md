## Why Good Packing Starts Long Before the Boxes

Most people underestimate how long packing a home really takes, and that is where moves turn stressful. The single most useful of all packing house tips is to start earlier than feels necessary — several weeks before your moving date rather than the final weekend. When you give yourself that runway, the work stops feeling like a wall to climb and becomes a series of small, achievable tasks. Packing a few boxes each evening keeps the job manageable and gives you room to make calm decisions rather than rushed ones, and it means no single day has to carry the whole burden.

Before a single box is taped shut, it helps to sort. There is little point carefully wrapping items you no longer want, only to unwrap them in a home where they do not belong. Working through what to keep, pass on or let go is a job in itself, and for anyone moving after decades in the same house it can be the hardest part. It is as much an emotional task as a practical one, which is exactly why rushing it tends to backfire. ## Packing Room by Room: The Method That Keeps It Calm

Trying to pack a whole house at once is overwhelming; packing one room at a time is not. Begin with the rooms you use least — spare bedrooms, lofts, dining rooms, the backs of cupboards — and leave kitchens, bathrooms and bedrooms until last, because you will need them right up to moving day. Finish each room completely before moving on, so you always know where you stand and can see steady progress rather than a half-done muddle spread across the house. That sense of completed ground is what keeps the process feeling calm instead of chaotic.

A few practical habits make everything easier on both ends of the move, saving time and frustration when you come to unpack:

- **Use the right boxes.** Double-walled cartons in mixed sizes, with heavy items in small boxes and light ones in large boxes, so nothing becomes too heavy to lift safely.
- **Protect as you go.** Wrap fragile items individually in paper or bubble wrap, and fill gaps so nothing shifts in transit.
- **Label clearly.** Mark every box with the room it belongs in and a note of its contents, and flag anything fragile.
- **Pack an essentials box.** Kettle, mugs, tea, medication, chargers, toiletries and a change of clothes — the things you will want within the first hour of arriving.

## Extra Care for Later-Life and Family-Arranged Moves

Packing takes on a different character when the move involves an older relative, a long-held family home, or sorting a property on someone else's behalf. The physical side — reaching high shelves, lifting boxes, standing for hours — is not realistic for everyone, and pushing through it can turn an already demanding week into something genuinely unsafe. The emotional side of handling a lifetime's belongings deserves time and patience rather than a rushed deadline, because every object carries a memory and deciding its future cannot always be done quickly.

In these situations the goal is not simply to get everything into boxes, but to do it with care: keeping track of important documents, protecting items of real sentimental value, and making sure medication and daily essentials are never packed away too soon. Those small safeguards matter more than speed, and they are easy to overlook when the clock is ticking. ## Frequently asked questions

### How early should I start packing before a move?

Begin several weeks ahead, starting with rooms and items you rarely use. Packing a little each day is far less stressful than cramming it all into the final weekend, and it leaves time to declutter properly.

### What packing materials do I actually need?

Double-walled cartons in a few sizes, bubble wrap or packing paper, strong tape, marker pens and labels. Specialist boxes help for wardrobes, pictures and crockery. Chayce Properties can supply and manage all materials as part of a packing service.

### How should I label my boxes?

Mark each box with the room it belongs in and a short note of contents. Flag fragile boxes and the ones you'll want first. Clear labelling means everything lands in the right room and nothing essential goes missing on day one.
