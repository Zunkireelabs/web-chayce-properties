**Short answer:** when you live far from a parent who needs to move, decide the plan together, put one person in charge of the practical work, and agree how and when you will be kept updated. Sort out who can make decisions early, and give yourselves more time than you think you need.

## Start with your parent, not the logistics

It is your parent's move. Before anything is booked, talk through why they are moving, where to, and what matters most to them: a garden, being near friends, keeping certain furniture. Our guide on [how to talk to elderly parents about moving](/blog/how-to-talk-to-elderly-parents-about-moving/) has ideas for that conversation.

If brothers, sisters or other relatives are involved, agree early who does what. A short shared list of jobs, with one name against each, avoids gaps and arguments later.

## Decide who can make decisions

If your parent can make their own decisions, they stay in charge and you support them. It is still worth asking whether they have, or want, a **lasting power of attorney**, so that someone they trust can act for them if that changes. [GOV.UK explains how to make one](https://www.gov.uk/power-of-attorney), and we cover it in [lasting power of attorney and moving a parent](/blog/lasting-power-of-attorney-moving-a-parent/).

Tell anyone helping with the move who the decision-makers are from the start.

## Put one person in charge of the practical side

Distance is hardest when nobody owns the day-to-day work. Either one family member who lives nearby takes it on, or you use a service where [one coordinator manages the whole move](/elderly-move-manager-uk/): sorting, packing, the removal, cleaning and setting up the new home.

Whoever it is, ask them for:

- a written plan with dates and what happens on each day
- a single phone number for you and for your parent
- an agreed time for updates, such as a call at the start and end of moving day

## Make the most of your visits

If you can visit once or twice, use those days for the things only family can do: deciding what to keep, sorting photographs and papers, and being there for your parent. Leave the lifting, packing and paperwork for the people doing the move.

Our [downsizing checklist](/blog/downsizing-checklist-what-to-keep-uk/) helps you decide what goes to the new home before the packing starts.

## Know where to find local help

Your parent's local council can tell you about care assessments, support at home and equipment. [Find their council on GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/find-local-council). [Age UK](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/information-advice/care/helping-a-loved-one/) and [Carers UK](https://www.carersuk.org/) have advice for families supporting an older relative.
