**Short answer:** when you buy a home in England or Northern Ireland you may pay Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) on the price of the new property, Scotland charges Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT), and Wales charges Land Transaction Tax (LTT). Downsizers usually pay less because the new home is cheaper, but there is no general downsizer discount in England, and buying before you sell can trigger an extra charge. Always check current rates. This is general information, not tax advice.

## Which tax applies where

- **England and Northern Ireland:** [Stamp Duty Land Tax](https://www.gov.uk/stamp-duty-land-tax).
- **Scotland:** [Land and Buildings Transaction Tax](https://revenue.scot/taxes/land-buildings-transaction-tax).
- **Wales:** Land Transaction Tax.

Tax is paid on the property you buy, not the one you sell. GOV.UK publishes the current [residential property rates](https://www.gov.uk/stamp-duty-land-tax/residential-property-rates), which change from time to time, so check them rather than relying on a figure from a guide.

## Buying before you have sold

If you buy a new home while still owning your old one, an extra charge for additional properties can apply. GOV.UK explains the rules in [buying an additional residential property](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/stamp-duty-land-tax-buying-an-additional-residential-property), including how it can be reclaimed when you sell your previous main home within a set time. Scotland and Wales have their own equivalent additional-property rules, with their own time limits.

If you are timing a sale and a purchase, tell your solicitor early so they can plan around this.
