**Short answer:** a moving consultation is a planning session you have before you move. You talk through your situation, and you leave with a written plan, a recommended timeline and a budget. It suits anyone who is moving, or helping a family member move, and is not sure what support they need. At Chayce Properties it is called the [Discovery Consultation](/discovery/): one hour, in person or by phone, £150.

## Why have a consultation first

A later-life move has many moving parts: sorting and downsizing, packing, the removal itself, the sale and purchase, and settling in. Guessing the scope from a room count often means paying for the wrong level of help. A consultation looks at what your move actually involves, so the support you choose fits the real job. If you are still deciding whether to move at all, it is a way to explore your options without committing to anything.

## What happens in the consultation

The Chayce Discovery Consultation has two parts:

- **Understanding your situation.** We talk through your current home, your timeline, your family circumstances and what is actually driving the move.
- **Walking through your options.** We assess which level of support genuinely fits, from a straightforward packing-and-removal approach through to full concierge-level support, and explain why.

## What you take away

You leave with three things in writing, not a verbal estimate:

1. A personalised move plan covering what needs to happen and when.
2. A recommended timeline.
3. A budget breakdown, so you know what to expect before you commit.

## How to prepare

You do not need everything sorted. A few notes make the hour more useful:

- **Your dates.** Any dates you already have, such as a sale agreed, a completion date, a care home place or a tenancy ending. If you have none yet, say so.
- **Where you are moving to**, or the kind of home you are considering, such as a bungalow, retirement flat or care home.
- **Who is involved.** Family members, your estate agent and your solicitor. Our [guide to who can help an older person move](/blog/who-can-help-an-older-person-move-home-uk/) lists the people to think about.
- **Your worries.** Stairs, sentimental items, health, a tight budget or a short timescale. Naming them early shapes the plan.
- **A rough idea of the home.** How many rooms, how much is in the loft, garage or shed, and what you already know you will keep. Our [downsizing checklist](/blog/downsizing-checklist-what-to-keep-uk/) can help.
