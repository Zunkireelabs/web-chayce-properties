Short answer: after a death, take things in order. Understand your role and the legal steps first, then sort belongings room by room with the family, and only then pack and move what is being kept. Chayce helps with the practical side in Northampton and across the UK, but does not give legal advice.

## Start with the legal side, not the house

GOV.UK explains [wills, probate and inheritance](https://www.gov.uk/wills-probate-inheritance) and what to do after a death in its [death and bereavement guidance](https://www.gov.uk/browse/births-deaths-marriages/death). If you are the executor and unsure what you can do and when, speak to a solicitor before removing or disposing of anything.

## Then sort, together

Once you know where you stand, work room by room with the family, deciding what is kept, passed on, donated or sold. Nothing needs to be rushed, and sentimental items deserve time. Our guide to [moving after probate and clearing a loved one's home](/blog/moving-after-probate-clearing-a-loved-ones-home/) goes through this step by step.

## Moving a surviving partner

Sometimes the practical job is also moving someone who is still living in the home. [Age UK](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/) has support for people after bereavement, and our guide to [moving an elderly parent](/moving-elderly-parent/) covers planning a move carefully.
