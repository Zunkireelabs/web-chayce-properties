**Short answer:** before anyone helps an older person move, check who they are, get the price and what is included in writing, ask about insurance and staff checks, and never agree to anything or hand over money on the doorstep. Involve family or a trusted adviser in the decision.

## Why this matters for later-life moves

A move means people coming into a home, handling belongings and often handling money. Older people are a common target for doorstep and cold-call scams, which [Age UK describes in detail](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/information-advice/money-legal/scams-fraud/doorstep-scams/). A few simple checks protect your parent and their belongings.

## Checks to make before you book

- **A real name and address.** The company should give its full name, a postal address and a landline or mobile you can call back.
- **Company registration.** If it is a limited company, [search the Companies House register](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/) for the name and company number.
- **Insurance.** Ask what insurance they hold for your belongings in transit and for accidents in the home, and ask for the details in writing.
- **Staff checks.** Ask whether the people coming into the home are DBS-checked and to what level. [The Disclosure and Barring Service](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/disclosure-and-barring-service) runs these checks in England and Wales.
- **A written quote.** Get the price, what it includes and the payment terms in writing before you agree to anything. Our guide on [how to compare removals quotes](/blog/how-to-compare-removals-quotes-uk-what-to-check/) covers what to look for.
- **Reviews and recommendations.** Read reviews on more than one site and ask friends, a care home or your solicitor who they recommend.

## Warning signs

- Someone turns up uninvited, or calls out of the blue, offering to clear or move a home.
- Pressure to decide today, or a "special price" that ends soon.
- A request for cash, or full payment before anything is in writing.
- No address, no company name, or a phone number that never answers.
- Offers to take "unwanted" valuables away for free.

If any of these happen, stop and talk to someone you trust. [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/scams/) explains how to check and report a scam.

## Keep the family involved

Agree that no one signs or pays for anything without a second person seeing it first. If someone holds a [lasting power of attorney](/blog/lasting-power-of-attorney-moving-a-parent/), make sure the company knows who they are.
