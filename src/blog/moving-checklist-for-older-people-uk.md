**Short answer:** an older person's move goes best when it is planned early, at a gentle pace, with the paperwork, medication and health arrangements sorted before the boxes. Use the timeline below: start three to six months ahead, keep medication and papers together, register with a GP surgery in the new area, check access at both ends and decide who will help with the heavy work.

*General information, not medical, legal or financial advice.*

## Three to Six Months Before

- **Decide where, and when.** Our guide to [when to downsize](/blog/when-to-downsize-uk/) and Age UK's guide to [housing options in later life](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/information-advice/care/housing-options/) can help if the choice is still open.
- **Think about paperwork and decisions.** A lasting power of attorney lets a person appoint someone to help make decisions for them (see [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/power-of-attorney)). Take legal advice early, and read our guide to [Lasting Power of Attorney and moving a parent](/blog/lasting-power-of-attorney-moving-a-parent/).
- **Talk about the plan.** Our guide to [talking to elderly parents about moving](/blog/how-to-talk-to-elderly-parents-about-moving/) helps.
- **Check the money first.** Take regulated advice before releasing money from a home. See [downsizing and equity release](/blog/downsizing-and-equity-release-what-to-know/).

## Two Months Before

- **Start sorting,** one category at a time, using our [downsizing checklist](/blog/downsizing-checklist-what-to-keep-uk/).
- **Decide who is helping.** Family, friends, your council, Age UK or a specialist. See [who can help an older person move home](/blog/who-can-help-an-older-person-move-home-uk/).
- **Get removals quotes** that include packing. Our guide to [choosing a removals company](/blog/choosing-removals-company-for-elderly-person-uk/) lists the questions to ask.
- **Check access at both ends:** stairs, steps, lifts and parking, and whether the new home suits current needs. See [adapting your home](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/information-advice/care/housing-options/adapting-home/) from Age UK.

## One Month Before

- **Register with a GP surgery in the new area** if you are moving away. The NHS says you can register or change surgery for free, and most surgeries accept online registration. See [how to register](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/gps/how-to-register-with-a-gp-surgery/).
- **Ask the GP or pharmacy about repeat prescriptions** and keep a current medication list.
- **Start the address changes,** using our [change of address checklist](/blog/change-of-address-checklist-uk/).
- **Pack non-essentials** by destination room, and label clearly.

## The Final Week

- **Pack a first-night box:** kettle, toiletries, a change of clothes, bedding, medication and chargers.
- **Keep medication, documents and valuables together** in one bag that travels with the person.
- **Confirm the moving day plan.** See our [moving day checklist](/blog/moving-day-checklist-uk/).
- **Arrange a quiet place** for the person to be while the van is loaded.

## After the Move

- **Set up the bed and main living area first,** with familiar things close by.
- **Keep routines going** and find the nearest shop, pharmacy and GP.
- **Ask for support** if it is needed. See our guide to [settling-in help for older people](/blog/settling-in-help-for-older-people-after-a-move-uk/).
