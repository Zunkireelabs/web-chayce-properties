**Short answer:** measure the new home, sketch it to scale, and place the large pieces first. Whatever has no place becomes a decision: offer it to family, sell it, donate it, recycle it or book a collection. Doing this before packing saves time, money and stress on moving day.

## Step 1: measure the new home

Get a floor plan from the seller or agent, or measure each room yourself. Note doors, windows, radiators and sockets, because they limit where furniture can go. Measure doorways, stairs and hallways too, so large pieces can actually get in.

## Step 2: measure what you own

List the big items you would like to keep: bed, sofa, wardrobe, dining table, favourite chair. Write down the width, depth and height of each.

## Step 3: sketch a simple plan

Draw each room on squared paper, with each square representing a fixed distance. Cut out paper shapes for the furniture and move them around. Leave clear walking space, especially if mobility may change.

## Step 4: decide what does not fit

Anything without a place is a decision. Make it once, using the keep, sell, donate and let-go piles in our [downsizing checklist](/blog/downsizing-checklist-what-to-keep-uk/). For pacing, see [how to declutter a parent's house](/blog/how-to-declutter-a-parents-house/).

## What to do with furniture that won't fit

**Family and friends first.** Ask whether anyone would like particular pieces. This avoids selling something a relative would have wanted and gives them time to arrange collection.

**Selling.** Good-quality or antique pieces may sell through local auction houses or online marketplaces. Selling takes time and not everything sells, so set a deadline. Keep photographs and measurements ready.

**Donating.** Many charities and charity shops accept furniture in good condition, and some offer collection. Ask what they accept, as there can be fire-safety rules for upholstered items. Donation is often quicker than selling.

**Recycling and collection.** Many councils offer bulky waste collection, sometimes for a fee, and some recycling centres take furniture. Check your council's website for current details, and book early because collections can have waiting times.

## Step 5: plan the first day

Mark which room each box and piece of furniture will go to, so the removal team can place everything straight away. Chayce labels boxes by destination room and, with Silver Comfort and above, unpacks and sets up the new home.
